# Digital Governance And Operating Model

## Nueva Voz Colectiva Ecosystem 2026-2036

## 1. Executive Summary

This document defines the daily operating model for Nueva Voz Colectiva Ecosystem between 2026 and 2036. It does not replace the Governance Model. The Governance Model defines institutional authority, governing bodies, directorates, and decision rights. This document defines how the digital and institutional ecosystem operates every day through workflows, lifecycle management, approvals, documentation, quality control, knowledge management, risk management, multilingual coordination, territorial nodes, and long-term continuity.

Nueva Voz Colectiva must operate as an international civic and territorial development organization, not as a small foundation, personal initiative, campaign, or founder-dependent project. Its operating model must make work traceable, reviewable, transferable, and scalable.

The model is built around a standard institutional flow:

```text
Proposal -> Review -> Approval -> Publication -> Monitoring -> Evaluation -> Historical Archive
```

This flow applies to programs, projects, publications, research, events, calls, courses, certifications, cooperation initiatives, impact reports, transparency outputs, and future territorial nodes.

## 2. Operating Principles

The daily operating model must follow these principles:

- Institution before individuals.
- Documentation before memory.
- Review before publication.
- Evidence before claims.
- Role clarity before growth.
- Traceability before scale.
- Continuity before speed.
- Quality before volume.
- Public trust before visibility.
- Cooperation readiness before fundraising.
- Territorial accountability before expansion.
- Shared responsibility before founder dependence.

### 3.1 Core Operating Flow

Every institutional initiative should follow the same operating flow:

```text
1. Proposal
2. Initial classification
3. Strategic alignment review
4. Technical or editorial review
5. Risk review
6. Approval
7. Production or implementation
8. Publication or launch
9. Monitoring
10. Evaluation
11. Lessons learned
12. Historical archive
```

### 3.2 Operating Rhythm

Recommended rhythm:

- Weekly: operational coordination.
- Monthly: program, project, content, and risk review.
- Quarterly: strategic portfolio review.
- Semiannual: documentation and quality audit.
- Annual: institutional report, impact review, transparency review, and operating model update.

## 4. Authorization Levels

### 4.1 Level 0: Drafting Authority

Can create proposals, drafts, notes, concepts, and working documents.

Typical roles:

- Program team.
- Project team.
- Content team.
- Research team.
- Event team.
- Territorial node team.

Cannot publish or formally approve.

### 4.2 Level 1: Operational Approval

Can approve low-risk operational actions within an already approved program or project.

Typical roles:

- Program owner.
- Project manager.
- Content lead.
- Event lead.
- Library lead.

Examples:

- Event agenda updates.
- Resource drafts for review.
- Routine program communications.
- Internal documentation updates.

### 4.3 Level 2: Institutional Approval

Required for public-facing institutional content, new projects, external communications, partner-facing materials, and transparency outputs.

Typical roles:

- Executive Direction.
- Relevant institutional director.
- Communications and Brand Direction.
- Digital Ecosystem Direction.

### 4.4 Level 3: Strategic Approval

Required for new programs, major cooperation initiatives, public institutional positions, high-risk partnerships, national expansion, international work, and major budget commitments.

Typical roles:

- Executive Direction.
- Strategic Council review when applicable.
- Board or highest institutional authority when required.

### 4.5 Level 4: Restricted Approval

Required for legal, financial, data protection, ethical, or reputationally sensitive matters.

Typical roles:

- Executive Direction.
- Legal or compliance advisor when available.
- Finance and Sustainability lead.
- Transparency lead.
- Board or highest authority when required.

## 6. Program Lifecycle

### 6.1 How A Program Is Born

A program begins when a recurring institutional need or strategic priority requires a stable line of work.

Program origin sources:

- Strategic plan.
- Community need.
- Territorial diagnosis.
- Educational priority.
- Cooperation opportunity.
- Research finding.
- Impact evaluation.
- Strategic Council recommendation.

### 6.2 Program Flow

```text
Proposal -> Strategic review -> Program design -> Risk review -> Approval -> Launch -> Monitoring -> Evaluation -> Renewal or archive
```

### 6.3 Required Program Documents

- Program concept note.
- Strategic alignment note.
- Audience definition.
- Methodology.
- Operating plan.
- Budget or resource estimate.
- Risk register.
- Monitoring indicators.
- Communication brief.
- Evaluation framework.

### 6.4 Program Approval

New programs require Level 3 Strategic Approval.

Routine program updates require Level 1 or Level 2 approval depending on public visibility and risk.

## 34. Strategic Recommendations

1. Use the standard lifecycle for every major institutional asset.
2. Require approval records for programs, projects, publications, calls, courses, certifications, cooperation, and transparency outputs.
3. Build operating templates before scaling activity volume.
4. Assign owners and backup reviewers for every critical function.
5. Maintain a decision log and risk register from the first year.
6. Keep Library, Observatory, Strategic Thinking Center, Campus, Community, Impact, and Transparency operationally distinct.
7. Treat Cooperation and Sustainability as one integrated operating area.
8. Develop English operations gradually and only for strategic content.
9. Pilot territorial nodes before formalizing them.
10. Review the operating model annually through 2036.
