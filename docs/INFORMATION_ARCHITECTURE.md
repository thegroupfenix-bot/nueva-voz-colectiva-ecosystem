# Information Architecture

## Nueva Voz Colectiva Ecosystem

## 1. Executive Summary

The information architecture of Nueva Voz Colectiva Ecosystem defines the institutional information system that will support leadership, education, citizen participation, territorial development, knowledge production, cooperation, transparency, and future digital platforms.

This document does not define visual navigation or interface design. It defines the entities, relationships, taxonomies, classifications, metadata, documentary organization, search models, filtering models, and knowledge management logic required for national and international growth between 2026 and 2036.

The architecture must support current institutional sections and future capabilities such as Campus Nueva Voz, Observatory, Community, Certifications, Calls Platform, Advanced Digital Library, Territorial Laboratories, and International Cooperation operations.

## 2. Information Architecture Principles

The information system must follow these principles:

- Institutional clarity.
- Entity consistency.
- Metadata discipline.
- Territorial traceability.
- Program and project accountability.
- Separation between knowledge, observatory, campus, cooperation, and transparency.
- Reusable content structures.
- Multilingual readiness for future international work.
- Evidence-based organization.
- Scalability for national and Latin American expansion.
- Searchability before volume growth.
- Governance before publication growth.

## 4. Entity Relationships

Information value comes from relationships between entities.

### 4.1 Program To Project

Relationship:

- A program may contain many projects.
- A project should belong to at least one program or strategic line.

Purpose:

- Connect institutional strategy with implementation.

### 4.2 Program To Course

Relationship:

- A program may generate one or more courses.
- A course may support one or more programs.

Purpose:

- Convert program knowledge into structured learning.

### 4.3 Course To Certification

Relationship:

- A course may be part of a certification path.
- A certification may require multiple courses, assessments, or activities.

Purpose:

- Support future Campus Nueva Voz and credentialing.

### 4.4 Project To Territory

Relationship:

- A project must be linked to one or more territories.

Purpose:

- Enable territorial traceability, reporting, and geographic filtering.

### 4.5 Project To Ally

Relationship:

- A project may involve one or more allies.

Purpose:

- Document partnerships and cooperation.

### 4.6 Project To Donor

Relationship:

- A project may be funded or supported by one or more donors.

Purpose:

- Support accountability and transparency.

### 4.7 Publication To Program

Relationship:

- A publication may support, document, or result from a program.

Purpose:

- Connect knowledge production to institutional work.

### 4.8 Research To Publication

Relationship:

- Research may produce publications, reports, studies, datasets, or methodologies.

Purpose:

- Preserve the chain from inquiry to published knowledge.

### 4.9 Indicator To Program Or Project

Relationship:

- Indicators may track programs, projects, territories, or institutional goals.

Purpose:

- Support monitoring, observatory outputs, and impact reporting.

### 4.10 Event To Program Or Project

Relationship:

- Events may belong to programs, projects, campaigns, or cooperation activities.

Purpose:

- Avoid isolated event archives and connect activities to outcomes.

### 4.11 Call To Program, Project, Event, Or Volunteer Role

Relationship:

- A call may invite applications to a program, project, event, volunteer opportunity, or partnership.

Purpose:

- Support structured participation and opportunity management.

### 4.12 Participant To Program, Course, Event, Or Volunteer Role

Relationship:

- A participant may enroll, attend, volunteer, complete courses, or join communities.

Purpose:

- Support future community, campus, volunteer, and certification models.

## 23. Information Architecture Risks

### 23.1 Entity Confusion

Risk: programs, projects, events, publications, and campaigns may be mixed without clear boundaries.

Mitigation:

- Define entity types and metadata before scaling.

### 23.2 Weak Metadata

Risk: content may become impossible to search, filter, or report.

Mitigation:

- Require metadata for every institutional asset.

### 23.3 Territorial Ambiguity

Risk: national and international expansion may become disorganized.

Mitigation:

- Use a territory taxonomy from the beginning.

### 23.4 Knowledge And Observatory Confusion

Risk: publications, datasets, indicators, and reports may be mixed incorrectly.

Mitigation:

- Separate library and knowledge management from observatory data systems.

### 23.5 Cooperation Reporting Risk

Risk: donor, ally, project, and report information may not connect properly.

Mitigation:

- Define cooperation entities and relationships early.

### 23.6 Privacy Risk

Risk: campus, community, volunteer, and participant data may be collected without sufficient controls.

Mitigation:

- Define consent, access levels, and data sensitivity classifications.

### 23.7 Content Sprawl

Risk: too many documents and resources may grow without ownership.

Mitigation:

- Assign owners, review cycles, and archive rules.

## 24. Strategic Recommendations

1. Treat information architecture as institutional infrastructure.
2. Define metadata before publishing large amounts of content.
3. Separate programs, projects, events, publications, and calls as distinct entities.
4. Connect all public content to strategic areas, audiences, and territories.
5. Build the library as the first knowledge access layer.
6. Keep the Observatory separate from the library and knowledge system.
7. Create partner, donor, and cooperation records before scaling international work.
8. Prepare for multilingual and multi-country metadata from the beginning.
9. Establish privacy and sensitivity classifications before launching campus, community, or volunteer platforms.
10. Review taxonomies annually as the organization grows.
