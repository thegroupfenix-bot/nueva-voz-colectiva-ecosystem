# Brand Positioning

## Nueva Voz Colectiva Ecosystem

## 1. Executive Summary

Nueva Voz Colectiva must be positioned as a civic, educational, territorial, and cooperation ecosystem. Its brand should communicate public trust, leadership formation, citizen participation, applied knowledge, and territorial development without being confused with a political party, electoral campaign, ideological platform, or assistance-based foundation.

The brand must represent a long-term institutional project: a citizen ecosystem that forms leaders, connects communities, produces knowledge, supports territorial development, and builds cooperation networks in Colombia, Latin America, and international contexts.

This positioning document defines the strategic identity of Nueva Voz Colectiva and provides communication criteria for future institutional, editorial, educational, territorial, and cooperation work.

## 2. Brand Purpose

Nueva Voz Colectiva exists to activate citizen capacity and collective leadership for territorial transformation.

The brand purpose is to help people, communities, and institutions move from concern to participation, from participation to leadership, and from leadership to collective impact.

The brand must make one idea clear: Nueva Voz Colectiva does not speak for citizens as a political representative; it creates spaces, knowledge, tools, and networks so citizens can learn, organize, collaborate, and act responsibly.

## 3. Institutional Positioning

Nueva Voz Colectiva is a civic ecosystem for leadership, education, participation, territorial development, strategic thinking, and cooperation.

It should be positioned as:

- A citizen ecosystem that connects people, communities, institutions, and ideas.
- A leadership platform that forms ethical, informed, and responsible civic leaders.
- A learning center that transforms knowledge into practical civic and territorial capacities.
- A territorial development organization that supports local agendas, projects, and community action.
- A cooperation network that connects local needs with national and international allies.
- A strategic thinking space that produces applied research, foresight, studies, and social innovation.

It should not be positioned as:

- A political party.
- An electoral campaign.
- A personal movement.
- An ideological platform.
- A charity-based or assistance-based foundation.
- A replacement for public institutions.

## 4. Differentiators

### Civic Independence

Nueva Voz Colectiva is not organized around electoral competition. Its legitimacy comes from civic education, leadership formation, public ethics, territorial work, and cooperation.

### Integrated Ecosystem Model

The brand combines education, leadership, participation, knowledge, observatory, community, cooperation, and sustainability into one long-term ecosystem.

### Territorial Orientation

The organization is rooted in real territorial needs. It must speak to neighborhoods, rural communities, municipalities, regions, and local institutions.

### Formation Before Mobilization

Nueva Voz Colectiva should prioritize education, deliberation, and capacity building before public action. Mobilization must be civic, non-electoral, and responsible.

### Knowledge And Evidence

The brand must be associated with studies, applied research, open knowledge, indicators, monitoring, reports, and learning resources.

### Cooperation Capacity

The organization must be credible to local communities and international partners at the same time. It should connect territorial realities with cooperation, alliances, and institutional support.

### Long-Term Institutional Horizon

The brand is built for sustained civic and territorial development, not for short-term visibility or campaign cycles.

## 6. Institutional Narrative

Nueva Voz Colectiva is born from a simple conviction: communities do not need to wait for others to imagine their future. They need knowledge, leadership, organization, trust, and cooperation to build it together.

Across Colombia and Latin America, many territories face complex challenges: low trust, limited participation, social fragmentation, weak civic education, lack of opportunities for young leaders, and disconnection between local needs and institutional resources. Nueva Voz Colectiva responds by creating a civic ecosystem where people can learn, lead, participate, research, cooperate, and transform their territories.

The organization does not seek to replace political institutions or compete in electoral spaces. It seeks to strengthen citizens, communities, and institutions through education, strategic thinking, territorial projects, public ethics, and cooperation.

Its promise is to help turn citizen energy into organized leadership, local concerns into shared agendas, knowledge into action, and cooperation into sustainable territorial impact.

## 7. Institutional Principles

### Independence

The brand must remain independent from political parties, electoral campaigns, candidates, and partisan structures.

### Public Trust

Every communication must strengthen transparency, credibility, and institutional consistency.

### Civic Responsibility

The brand must encourage informed participation, constructive dialogue, and shared responsibility.

### Territorial Respect

The organization must avoid imposing external formulas on communities. It must listen, learn, and work from territorial realities.

### Inclusion

The brand must reflect openness to diverse communities, generations, regions, and perspectives.

### Education

The organization must position education as a central path to leadership, participation, and development.

### Cooperation

The brand must communicate that transformation requires alliances among communities, institutions, academia, civil society, private actors, and international partners.

### Non-Assistance-Based Impact

Nueva Voz Colectiva must strengthen capacities and autonomy, not dependency.

### Evidence And Learning

The brand must value data, research, monitoring, reflection, and continuous improvement.

## 9. Communication Tone

The communication tone should be:

- Institutional but accessible.
- Civic but non-partisan.
- Inspiring but realistic.
- Educational but not academicist.
- Strategic but understandable.
- Territorial but not localist in a limiting way.
- Internationally credible but rooted in community realities.
- Warm but not informal to the point of losing trust.

Preferred tone examples:

- "We strengthen citizen leadership."
- "We connect knowledge, communities, and cooperation."
- "We build capacities for territorial transformation."
- "We promote informed and responsible participation."
- "We support communities with education, evidence, and alliances."

## 11. Prohibited Language

Avoid language that creates political, ideological, electoral, or assistance-based confusion:

- Movement for power.
- Electoral project.
- Political machinery.
- Campaign structure.
- Vote for change.
- Our candidate.
- Take power.
- Defeat opponents.
- Ideological front.
- Partisan base.
- Beneficiaries as passive recipients.
- Charity for the poor.
- Saving communities.
- Giving voice to those who have none.
- We represent the people.
- We are the only alternative.
- Permanent campaign.

Avoid excessive hero language centered on one person:

- Founder as savior.
- Leader who will rescue the territory.
- Personal movement.
- The only leader capable of change.

Avoid polarizing language:

- Enemies.
- Traitors.
- Us against them.
- Good citizens versus bad citizens.
- Old politics versus pure citizens.

Preferred replacement:

- Use "communities", "participants", "leaders", "partners", "allies", "members", and "territorial actors" instead of passive or polarizing labels.

The national positioning should emphasize:

- Colombia's territorial diversity.
- Youth and emerging leadership.
- Community organization.
- Civic education.
- Local development.
- Public trust.
- Participation beyond electoral cycles.
- Institutional collaboration.
- Knowledge and evidence for territorial decisions.

Recommended national positioning statement:

"Nueva Voz Colectiva is a Colombian civic ecosystem that forms leaders, strengthens citizen participation, produces knowledge, and connects communities and institutions for territorial development."

## 13. International Positioning

Internationally, Nueva Voz Colectiva should be positioned as a Colombian and Latin American civic cooperation platform focused on leadership, education, participation, strategic thinking, and territorial development.

The international positioning should emphasize:

- Colombia as a territory of civic innovation and community resilience.
- Latin America as a shared space for democratic learning and territorial cooperation.
- International cooperation as a bridge between local realities and global resources.
- Evidence, transparency, and impact reporting.
- Partnerships with universities, foundations, cooperation agencies, NGOs, and social innovation networks.

Recommended international positioning statement:

"Nueva Voz Colectiva is a civic and territorial development ecosystem from Colombia, with Latin American projection, connecting leadership formation, applied knowledge, citizen participation, and international cooperation."

## 14. Future Brand Architecture

Nueva Voz Colectiva should use an endorsed ecosystem architecture. The main institutional brand remains the source of trust, while future initiatives operate as named sub-brands or programs under the same umbrella.

### Master Brand

- Nueva Voz Colectiva.

### Future Ecosystem Brands

- Campus Nueva Voz.
- Observatorio Nueva Voz.
- Biblioteca Digital Nueva Voz.
- Comunidad Nueva Voz.
- Certificaciones Nueva Voz.
- Laboratorios Territoriales Nueva Voz.
- Red de Liderazgo Nueva Voz.
- Cooperacion Nueva Voz.

### Brand Architecture Principle

Each future brand should strengthen the main institutional identity. No sub-brand should appear independent enough to fragment trust, duplicate governance, or create confusion about purpose.

Recommended structure:

- Nueva Voz Colectiva: institutional ecosystem.
- Campus Nueva Voz: education and certifications.
- Observatorio Nueva Voz: indicators, monitoring, data, and reports.
- Biblioteca Digital Nueva Voz: resources, publications, guides, and knowledge.
- Comunidad Nueva Voz: members, volunteers, alumni, and civic networks.
- Cooperacion Nueva Voz: alliances, donors, calls, and international projects.

## 15. Reputational Risks

### Political Confusion

Risk: audiences may interpret the organization as a party, campaign, or pre-electoral platform.

Mitigation: use civic, educational, and institutional language consistently; avoid candidate-centered communication.

### Ideological Labeling

Risk: the brand may be framed as a partisan or ideological project.

Mitigation: emphasize public ethics, democratic participation, evidence, plurality, and civic independence.

### Assistance-Based Perception

Risk: the organization may be perceived as charity or short-term aid.

Mitigation: communicate capacity building, education, leadership, autonomy, and territorial co-responsibility.

### Founder Dependence

Risk: the brand may become too dependent on one person.

Mitigation: build institutional voice, governance, teams, programs, and community narratives.

### Overpromising

Risk: promising campus, observatory, international cooperation, certifications, and territorial transformation before operational capacity exists.

Mitigation: communicate phases, pilots, learning, and progressive development.

### Cooperation Credibility Risk

Risk: international partners may require stronger evidence, governance, reporting, and financial transparency than the organization initially has.

Mitigation: build documentation, reporting standards, measurable indicators, and transparent project portfolios.

### Content Inconsistency

Risk: different messages across programs and channels may weaken identity.

Mitigation: adopt editorial guidelines, approved vocabulary, and communication review processes.

### Polarization Risk

Risk: public issues can easily pull the brand into conflict dynamics.

Mitigation: communicate from civic education, dialogue, evidence, and territorial solutions.

## 16. Communication Recommendations

1. Lead with purpose, not with personalities.
2. Use institutional and civic language consistently.
3. Avoid campaign-style slogans and electoral visual codes.
4. Explain clearly that participation is civic, educational, and territorial.
5. Present communities as active agents, not passive beneficiaries.
6. Build trust through transparency, documentation, and reporting.
7. Separate opinion, research, institutional statements, and educational content.
8. Use evidence and territorial learning in public communication.
9. Communicate future initiatives as phased capabilities, not immediate promises.
10. Develop a shared glossary for all institutional channels.
11. Use the master brand to endorse future sub-brands.
12. Maintain a clear distinction between knowledge, observatory, campus, cooperation, and community.
13. Prepare specific narratives for Colombia, Latin America, and international cooperation partners.
14. Review public communications for political ambiguity before publication.
15. Measure reputation through audience feedback, partner confidence, media mentions, and institutional trust indicators.

## 17. Strategic Positioning Decisions

The brand positioning adopts the following decisions:

1. Nueva Voz Colectiva is a civic ecosystem, not a party or campaign.
2. The brand will prioritize leadership formation, education, territorial development, and cooperation.
3. The institutional voice will be collective, not personality-centered.
4. The brand will avoid ideological, electoral, and polarizing language.
5. The organization will communicate communities as partners and participants, not passive beneficiaries.
6. National positioning will focus on Colombia's civic and territorial development needs.
7. International positioning will focus on Latin American projection, cooperation, applied knowledge, and impact.
8. Future sub-brands will remain endorsed by the main institutional brand.
