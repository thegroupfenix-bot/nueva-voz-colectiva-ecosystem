# Governance Model

## Nueva Voz Colectiva Ecosystem

## 1. Executive Summary

Nueva Voz Colectiva requires a governance model that allows the organization to begin with a small, focused structure and mature into a national civic, educational, territorial, and cooperation ecosystem with international projection.

The governance model must protect institutional independence, avoid electoral or partisan capture, support transparent decision-making, enable program and project execution, and create accountability mechanisms for communities, partners, donors, and institutional allies.

This document defines the organizational structure, governing bodies, strategic council, institutional directorates, program and project management, international cooperation governance, digital governance, documentary management, roles, responsibilities, risk management, transparency practices, decision-making model, and organizational evolution from 2026 to 2036.

## 2. Governance Principles

The governance of Nueva Voz Colectiva must be guided by the following principles:

- Civic independence.
- Institutional transparency.
- Public ethics.
- Non-partisan operation.
- Territorial relevance.
- Educational rigor.
- Evidence-based decision-making.
- Accountability.
- Cooperation and partnership.
- Operational scalability.
- Documentation discipline.
- Inclusion and plural participation.
- Separation between strategy, execution, supervision, and evaluation.

## 3. Organizational Structure

Nueva Voz Colectiva should evolve through a modular organizational structure. In the first phase, roles may be held by a small team. Over time, responsibilities should become formal directorates, units, committees, and territorial nodes.

### 3.1 Initial Structure

Recommended initial structure:

- Executive Leadership.
- Strategic Council.
- Program Coordination.
- Project Coordination.
- Communications and Content Coordination.
- Digital and Documentation Coordination.
- Cooperation and Partnerships Coordination.
- Administration and Transparency Support.

### 3.2 Mature Structure

Recommended mature structure:

- General Assembly or Institutional Board.
- Strategic Council.
- Executive Direction.
- Institutional Directorates.
- Program and Project Units.
- International Cooperation Unit.
- Digital Governance Unit.
- Territorial Nodes.
- Advisory Committees.
- Transparency and Accountability Function.

## 4. Governing Bodies

### 4.1 General Assembly Or Institutional Board

Purpose: serve as the highest institutional authority when the legal and organizational structure requires it.

Responsibilities:

- Approve institutional statutes and major reforms.
- Appoint or validate executive leadership according to the legal structure.
- Approve long-term institutional direction.
- Review annual institutional reports.
- Protect the civic, non-partisan, and non-assistance-based identity of the organization.

### 4.2 Executive Direction

Purpose: lead institutional execution, coordinate teams, represent the organization, and ensure alignment with strategy.

Responsibilities:

- Execute the approved strategic plan.
- Coordinate directorates and programs.
- Manage institutional priorities.
- Represent Nueva Voz Colectiva before partners and allies.
- Ensure compliance with governance, transparency, and documentation standards.
- Present periodic reports to the governing body and strategic council.

### 4.3 Strategic Council

Purpose: provide strategic orientation, external perspective, credibility, and long-term guidance.

The Strategic Council should be advisory, not operational. It should not replace executive leadership or become a partisan body.

Recommended profiles:

- Civic leadership expert.
- Education or pedagogy expert.
- Territorial development expert.
- International cooperation expert.
- Public policy or governance expert.
- Research and data expert.
- Social innovation expert.
- Communications or reputation expert.
- Financial sustainability expert.

Responsibilities:

- Advise on long-term strategy.
- Review institutional positioning.
- Identify strategic risks.
- Recommend partnerships.
- Support reputation and credibility.
- Review annual impact and transparency reports.
- Protect institutional independence.

### 4.4 Program Committee

Purpose: supervise programmatic coherence across education, leadership, participation, territory, knowledge, and community.

Responsibilities:

- Review program design.
- Validate learning objectives.
- Avoid duplication among programs.
- Ensure alignment with institutional principles.
- Define program evaluation criteria.

### 4.5 Project Committee

Purpose: review, prioritize, and monitor territorial, cooperation, research, and institutional projects.

Responsibilities:

- Approve project concepts before formulation.
- Review budgets, objectives, risks, and implementation plans.
- Monitor project delivery.
- Ensure documentation and reporting.
- Close projects with lessons learned.

### 4.6 Transparency And Accountability Committee

Purpose: strengthen public trust through oversight of reporting, documentation, financial transparency, and ethical compliance.

Responsibilities:

- Review transparency reports.
- Monitor conflict of interest declarations.
- Recommend accountability practices.
- Ensure publication of relevant institutional information.
- Review complaints or ethical concerns when applicable.

## 5. Strategic Council

The Strategic Council should operate as a high-level advisory body with scheduled sessions and documented recommendations.

### 5.1 Composition

Suggested size:

- Initial phase: 3 to 5 advisors.
- Consolidation phase: 5 to 7 advisors.
- National and international phase: 7 to 11 advisors.

### 5.2 Selection Criteria

Members should be selected based on:

- Professional credibility.
- Ethical reputation.
- Alignment with civic independence.
- Knowledge in relevant fields.
- Absence of direct electoral conflicts.
- Capacity to open institutional, academic, territorial, or cooperation networks.

### 5.3 Operating Model

Recommended operating rhythm:

- Quarterly strategic sessions.
- Annual strategy review.
- Special sessions for major risks or partnerships.
- Written recommendations after each session.

### 5.4 Boundaries

The Strategic Council should not:

- Direct daily operations.
- Endorse candidates or parties.
- Use the organization for personal political visibility.
- Approve content outside its advisory scope.
- Replace formal governance or legal responsibilities.

## 7. Program Management

Programs are ongoing institutional lines of work. They may contain projects, events, learning products, publications, and community activities.

### 7.1 Program Categories

Recommended program categories:

- Leadership and civic education.
- Citizen participation.
- Territorial development.
- Strategic thinking and applied research.
- Observatory and data.
- International cooperation.
- Community and volunteering.
- Campus and certifications in future phases.

### 7.2 Program Lifecycle

Each program should follow this lifecycle:

1. Strategic justification.
2. Audience definition.
3. Objectives and expected outcomes.
4. Methodology.
5. Resources and team.
6. Risk assessment.
7. Implementation plan.
8. Monitoring indicators.
9. Communication plan.
10. Evaluation and lessons learned.

### 7.3 Program Governance Requirements

Each program must have:

- Program owner.
- Approved objectives.
- Defined audience.
- Documented methodology.
- Budget or resource estimate.
- Impact indicators.
- Reporting rhythm.
- Public communication criteria.

## 8. Project Management

Projects are time-bound initiatives with specific objectives, resources, outputs, and reporting requirements.

### 8.1 Project Types

Recommended project types:

- Territorial projects.
- Educational projects.
- Cooperation projects.
- Research projects.
- Digital projects.
- Community projects.
- Events and campaigns of civic education.

### 8.2 Project Lifecycle

Each project should follow this lifecycle:

1. Concept note.
2. Alignment review.
3. Feasibility assessment.
4. Budget and resource plan.
5. Risk review.
6. Approval.
7. Execution.
8. Monitoring.
9. Reporting.
10. Closure and documentation.

### 8.3 Minimum Project Documentation

Every project should maintain:

- Project brief.
- Objectives.
- Stakeholder map.
- Work plan.
- Budget.
- Risk register.
- Monitoring indicators.
- Communication notes.
- Final report.
- Lessons learned.

## 9. International Cooperation Governance

International cooperation must be governed with rigor, transparency, and technical capacity. It should not be improvised as fundraising communication.

### 9.1 Cooperation Principles

- Alignment with institutional mission.
- Territorial relevance.
- Transparency.
- Ethical fundraising.
- Clear reporting.
- Respect for partner requirements.
- Avoidance of mission drift.
- No partisan or electoral use of cooperation resources.

### 9.2 Cooperation Workflow

Recommended workflow:

1. Partner or opportunity identification.
2. Strategic fit review.
3. Risk and eligibility assessment.
4. Concept note.
5. Budget and implementation design.
6. Approval by executive leadership and relevant committee.
7. Proposal submission.
8. Agreement review.
9. Implementation.
10. Donor reporting.
11. Public accountability summary.

### 9.3 Cooperation Controls

The organization should maintain:

- Partner database.
- Grant calendar.
- Cooperation portfolio.
- Proposal archive.
- Agreements archive.
- Donor reporting calendar.
- Restricted funding controls.
- Conflict of interest review.

### 10.1 Digital Assets

Digital assets include:

- Digital library.

- Observatory tools.
- Community platform.

Digital governance must define:

- Who can create content.

- Who can publish content.
- Who can manage user access.

- Who reviews security and privacy risks.

### 10.3 Digital Principles

## 11. Documentary Management

Documentation is a governance function, not a secondary administrative task.

### 11.1 Document Categories

Recommended categories:

- Institutional strategy.
- Brand and communication.
- Governance.
- Programs.
- Projects.
- Cooperation.
- Technical architecture.
- Content strategy.
- Research and knowledge.
- Observatory and data.
- Transparency reports.
- Policies and procedures.

### 11.2 Documentation Standards

Each institutional document should include:

- Title.
- Purpose.
- Scope.
- Owner.
- Version or date.
- Related documents.
- Review cycle.
- Approval status when applicable.

### 11.3 Review Rhythm

Recommended review rhythm:

- Strategic documents: annual review.
- Governance documents: annual review or when legal structure changes.
- Program documents: before each cycle and after evaluation.
- Project documents: at approval, midpoint, closure.
- Technical documents: every six months or after major platform changes.
- Transparency documents: according to reporting calendar.

## 12. Roles And Responsibilities

### 12.1 Role Matrix

| Role | Main Responsibility | Decision Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General Assembly or Board | Institutional authority | Statutes, major reforms, annual reports |
| Executive Direction | Strategy execution and representation | Operational priorities, team coordination |
| Strategic Council | Advisory guidance | Recommendations, risk perspective |
| Program Owner | Program design and delivery | Program operations within approved scope |
| Project Manager | Project execution | Project tasks, reporting, delivery |
| Cooperation Lead | Partnerships and donor relations | Cooperation pipeline and proposals |
| Digital Lead | Digital platforms and workflows | Platform operations and access management |
| Content Lead | Editorial planning and quality | Content calendar and publication drafts |
| Data or Observatory Lead | Indicators and monitoring | Data standards and reporting outputs |
| Finance Lead | Budget and sustainability controls | Financial reporting and budget tracking |
| Transparency Lead | Accountability and documentation | Public reporting and compliance tracking |

### 12.2 Separation Of Duties

Where possible:

- The person who proposes a project should not be the only person approving it.
- The person who manages funds should not be the only person reporting on them.
- The person who creates content should not be the only person approving public communication.
- The person who manages data should not publish interpretations without review.

In the initial phase, if one person holds multiple roles, decisions should still be documented and reviewed by at least one additional responsible actor.

## 13. Organizational Scaling

Nueva Voz Colectiva should scale in phases.

### 13.1 Phase 1: Foundational Team

Characteristics:

- Small team.
- Founder or executive leadership centrality.
- Basic documentation.
- Initial programs.
- Early partners.
- Manual workflows.

Governance priority:

- Avoid informality becoming the operating culture.

### 13.2 Phase 2: Coordinated Units

Characteristics:

- Program coordinators.
- Project leads.
- Communication and digital roles.
- Initial advisory council.
- Basic reporting.

Governance priority:

- Define approval flows and role clarity.

### 13.3 Phase 3: Institutional Directorates

Characteristics:

- Formal directorates.
- Program portfolio.
- Project management standards.
- Cooperation pipeline.
- Observatory and knowledge production.
- Stronger financial controls.

Governance priority:

- Separate strategy, execution, supervision, and reporting.

### 13.4 Phase 4: National Organization

Characteristics:

- Territorial nodes.
- Regional partnerships.
- National program reach.
- Public reporting.
- Digital maturity.
- Expanded team.

Governance priority:

- Maintain coherence across territories.

### 13.5 Phase 5: International Projection

Characteristics:

- International cooperation portfolio.
- Latin American partnerships.
- Multi-region knowledge production.
- Bilingual or multilingual materials.
- Advanced reporting.

Governance priority:

- Protect mission alignment while working with diverse funders and partners.

## 14. Risk Management

Risk management must be systematic and documented.

### 14.1 Strategic Risks

- Political or electoral confusion.
- Mission drift.
- Founder dependence.
- Loss of civic independence.
- Reputational damage.
- Unsustainable growth.

### 14.2 Operational Risks

- Lack of role clarity.
- Weak project management.
- Poor documentation.
- Inconsistent program quality.
- Team overload.
- Unclear approval processes.

### 14.3 Financial Risks

- Dependence on one funding source.
- Weak budget tracking.
- Misalignment between funding and mission.
- Insufficient reporting to donors.
- Lack of reserve planning.

### 14.4 Digital Risks

- Weak access controls.
- Unreviewed public content.
- Data privacy issues.
- Platform fragmentation.
- Loss of institutional knowledge.

### 14.5 Cooperation Risks

- Overpromising to international partners.
- Poor grant compliance.
- Weak reporting.
- Partner misalignment.
- Reputational exposure through alliances.

### 14.6 Risk Management Tools

Recommended tools:

- Risk register.
- Conflict of interest register.
- Decision log.
- Project approval checklist.
- Partner due diligence checklist.
- Incident response protocol.
- Quarterly risk review.

## 16. Decision-Making Model

Nueva Voz Colectiva should use a tiered decision model.

### 16.1 Strategic Decisions

Examples:

- Institutional direction.
- New major programs.
- Major partnerships.
- National expansion.
- International cooperation agreements.
- Brand positioning changes.

Decision path:

1. Executive proposal.
2. Strategic Council review when applicable.
3. Board or highest authority approval when required.
4. Documented decision.
5. Implementation plan.

### 16.2 Programmatic Decisions

Examples:

- Program design.
- Curriculum priorities.
- Target audiences.
- Program evaluation changes.

Decision path:

1. Program owner proposal.
2. Program Committee review.
3. Executive approval.
4. Documentation update.

### 16.3 Project Decisions

Examples:

- New territorial project.
- Grant proposal.
- Research project.
- Event series.

Decision path:

1. Concept note.
2. Project Committee review.
3. Budget and risk review.
4. Approval.
5. Monitoring and reporting.

### 16.4 Digital Decisions

Examples:

- Website structure changes.
- New digital platform.
- Data collection tools.
- Publishing workflows.

Decision path:

1. Digital or content proposal.
2. Technical and governance review.
3. Executive approval.
4. Documentation.
5. Access and maintenance plan.

### 16.5 Emergency Decisions

Emergency decisions may be required for reputational, security, legal, or operational risks.

Minimum requirements:

- Executive decision.
- Written record within 48 hours.
- Post-decision review.
- Corrective actions when needed.

### 2026-2027: Foundation

Governance focus:

- Define legal and institutional structure.
- Establish Executive Direction.
- Create initial Strategic Council.
- Document decision-making.
- Define brand and communication controls.
- Create basic transparency practices.

### 2028-2029: Consolidation

Governance focus:

- Formalize program and project management.
- Create Program and Project Committees.
- Establish cooperation workflow.
- Publish annual reports.
- Define role matrix.
- Improve financial controls.

### 2030-2031: Expansion

Governance focus:

- Create territorial coordination model.
- Develop regional partner governance.
- Formalize knowledge, observatory, and data functions.
- Strengthen monitoring and evaluation.
- Build project portfolio discipline.

### 2032-2033: Digital Maturity

Governance focus:

- Govern Campus Nueva Voz.
- Govern Digital Library.
- Govern Observatory.
- Govern Community platform.
- Define certification controls.
- Strengthen data protection and digital security.

### 2034-2036: National And International Projection

Governance focus:

- Expand Strategic Council with international profiles.
- Strengthen cooperation compliance.
- Operate national and Latin American partnerships.
- Publish advanced impact reports.
- Maintain mission alignment across growth.
- Prepare long-term sustainability and succession planning.

## 19. Strategic Recommendations

1. Begin with a lean structure but document responsibilities from the start.
2. Create the Strategic Council early, but keep it advisory.
3. Separate governance, execution, and accountability as soon as operational capacity allows.
4. Treat documentation as institutional infrastructure.
5. Avoid founder-centered governance by building role-based decision systems.
6. Create minimum standards for every program and project before scaling.
7. Build transparency practices before seeking large cooperation resources.
8. Keep international cooperation under strict approval, reporting, and compliance controls.
9. Establish digital governance before launching advanced platforms.
10. Review governance annually and after each major growth phase.
